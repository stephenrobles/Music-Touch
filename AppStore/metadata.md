**Promotional text (170):**

**Description (4000):**
Music Touch turns the touchscreen on your MacBook Pro into a piano. Turn it on from the menu bar and the whole display becomes nine note zones playing the C major scale, from middle C at the bottom up to the D above the octave at the top. Touch anywhere to play. Use several fingers for a chord, or slide a finger across the zones for a glissando. Every touch sends out a glowing ring in that note's colour.

The sound is a soft, reverberant grand piano with a gentle sustain and fade, the kind of tone you'd hear under a quiet film scene. How long each note rings is up to you.

HOW IT WORKS
• Click the piano keys in the menu bar, or press ⌃⌥⌘M anywhere, to turn Music Touch on or off
• While it's on, a transparent layer covers the screen so a touch never lands in the app underneath. Turn it off and everything is back to normal
• Nine zones: C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 B4 C5 D5, bottom to top. Switch to horizontal to put the low notes on the left and the high notes on the right
• Show separator lines with note names while you learn the layout, or hide them for a clean screen
• Several fingers play a chord. Dragging across a boundary plays the next note
• Set how long notes sustain and fade, from a short half second to a long eight-second wash
• Launch at login, if you like having it ready

SOUND
• The grand piano built into macOS, mellowed with a low-pass filter and a long hall reverb
• Every zone has its own voice, so notes overlap and fade on their own

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• No account, no analytics, no network access of any kind. Nothing leaves your Mac

Music Touch is made for the MacBook Pro with Touchscreen PROLED XDR Display, and it also works with a mouse or trackpad on any Mac running macOS 26 or later: click to play, drag for a glissando.

Music Touch is free and open source under the MIT license.

## App Review Information

- **Sign-in required:** No
- **Contact:** Stephen Robles, steve@beard.fm
- **Notes:**
  Music Touch is a menu bar utility (no Dock icon). After launching, click the piano-keys icon in the menu bar
  and choose "Music Touch On", or press Control-Option-Command-M. The screen is then covered by a transparent
  layer: clicking or touching anywhere plays a piano note and shows a coloured ring, and dragging plays a
  run of notes. The app is designed for the MacBook Pro with Touchscreen PROLED XDR Display, but every
  feature can be exercised with a mouse or trackpad: click to play, drag for a glissando. Turn it off from
  the menu bar or with the same shortcut to return to normal use. Settings (Command-comma from the menu)
  has orientation, separator lines, note length and launch at login. The app makes no network requests and
  collects no data. The sandboxed App Store build does not include the optional lid-logo helper offered in
  the direct-download version.
